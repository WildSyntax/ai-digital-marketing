Your best-selling product might be the one losing you money.

Most small stores rank products by revenue. A better ranking is contribution margin: the price minus every cost that grows with each order.

Take two products. A $60 jacket sells 200 a month, so $12,000 in revenue. After $56 of product cost, shipping, payment fees and ads per order, it leaves $4 a unit. That is $800 a month.

A $25 beanie sells 300 a month, so $7,500 in revenue. It costs $14 per order all in and needs very little ad spend. It leaves $11 a unit, or $3,300 a month.

The beanie earns four times as much on less revenue. A revenue report would tell you to push the jacket.

You can check your own store in an hour. Export last month's orders by product. Subtract product cost, shipping, payment fees and ad spend per order. Then sort by total contribution in dollars.

The products at the bottom of that list are where to raise prices, cut ad spend, or stop selling.
