## Script
### Hook (0:00–0:30)
You open your Shopify analytics, see a wall of charts, and close the tab. Sound familiar? Most store owners check the numbers when they feel anxious and ignore them the rest of the time. In this video I will show you a simple routine. Ten minutes, once a week, the same steps every time. By the end you will know what to look at and what to do about it.

### Set up your 10-minute routine
Before we look at any numbers, let's fix the habit. Pick one slot each week. Monday morning works well, because you can act on what you learn while the week is fresh. Put it in your calendar and treat it like a meeting with your store.

Next, set the date range. Compare the last seven days with the seven days before. A single week on its own tells you very little. A week next to the previous one tells you whether something moved.

Finally, open a notes file. You will write down numbers as you go, and you will write one decision at the end. If you skip the notes, the routine turns into browsing. Browsing feels like work, but it rarely changes anything in your store. The notes are what turn looking into deciding. Keep the file in the same place every week so you can scroll back and see the pattern over a month.

### Minutes 1 to 3: sales and orders
Start with total sales and number of orders. Only two numbers. Write both down next to last week's.

Now look at average order value. That is total sales divided by orders. If sales went up but orders stayed flat, your customers are spending more each time. If orders went up but sales stayed flat, you are getting more small baskets, and that may point to a discount or a cheap product pulling people in.

Do not chase every wiggle. A small change in one week is normal. Ask yourself one question: is this a move I would notice without the chart? If yes, flag it in your notes and keep going. If no, move on.

You are not trying to explain everything in these three minutes. You are only deciding where to look next, and that is a much easier job.

### Minutes 4 to 6: conversion rate
Next, conversion rate. This is the share of visits that end in an order. Say your store gets 1,000 visits and 20 orders. That is a 2% conversion rate. The numbers here are only an example, so use your own.

Why does this matter? Because it separates two very different problems. If your visits dropped, you have a traffic problem. If visits held steady but orders fell, you have a conversion problem. Those need different fixes, and mixing them up wastes weeks.

Look at conversion rate for mobile and desktop separately if your report allows it. Many stores find that phones bring most of the visits but fewer orders. If that is true for you, the checkout on a phone is the first place to test.

Write down the rate, write down whether visits or orders moved, and go to the next step.

### Minutes 7 to 9: products and traffic sources
Now we find out what is driving the numbers. Open the report that ranks your products by sales. Look at the top five. Are they the same as last week? If a product has climbed, ask why. Did you send an email, run an ad, or get a mention somewhere? If a product has dropped, check that it is still in stock and that the page still loads well.

Then open traffic sources. You want to know which channels send visits, and more importantly which ones send orders. A channel with lots of visits and few orders is a candidate for a closer look. A channel with few visits and steady orders may deserve more attention.

Keep it simple. You are matching a number you flagged earlier to a likely cause. You do not need a perfect answer, only a good guess you can test next week.

### Minute 10: write one decision
This is the most important step, and the one most people skip. Look at your notes and choose one thing to do this week. Just one.

It might be: rewrite the description on the product that is climbing. Or: test a shorter checkout on mobile. Or: pause the channel that sends visits but no orders. Write it as a sentence that starts with a verb and has a day attached to it.

Why only one? Because a list of seven ideas becomes a list of zero actions. One decision gets done, and next Monday you can see whether it moved a number.

That is the whole loop. Look, compare, find the cause, decide. Then close the tab and get back to running your store.

### Close
Here is the routine in three lines. Compare this week with last week. Check sales, conversion rate, then products and traffic. Write one decision and do it.
